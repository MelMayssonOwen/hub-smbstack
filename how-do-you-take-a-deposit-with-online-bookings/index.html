How Do You Take a Deposit With Online Bookings?

Published 2026-11-01

TL;DR: To take a deposit with online bookings, collect part of the payment when the customer books, make the deposit rule clear on the booking page, connect and test your payment setup, and confirm how the remaining balance will be handled later.

Set up deposits in the booking flow

Taking a deposit during an online booking means collecting part of the payment right when the customer books. You then spell out how the rest gets collected later. Simple concept, kind of a pain to set up, and totally standard business practice. The key is making sure the booking flow and payment flow line up so the customer knows exactly what they are paying now versus later.

Choose the deposit rule you want to enforce

Before flipping any switches, nail down what your actual deposit rule is. The big questions stay pretty straightforward:

  • Is the deposit a fixed amount or a percentage of the total?
  • Is it charged when the booking is submitted, or only after the appointment is confirmed?
  • Is it refundable under any circumstances?
  • If a customer cancels or reschedules, does the deposit stay on the booking, get credited, or get refunded?

Those choices form your policy, not some vague feeling. Write them down first. Software can only enforce the rule you actually picked.

Check whether your scheduling software supports deposits

Dig through the official docs for the booking features you need. A solid deposit setup usually requires payment collection at booking time, custom payment rules, cancellation options, receipts, and a clear calendar status showing whether the booking is confirmed after payment.

If the docs do not spell those pieces out, assume you do not actually have deposit support yet. Hidden settings are just surprise chores wearing a friendlier label.

Connect your payment processor and test the checkout path

Once the rule is set, link the payment account your scheduling software uses and map it to the booking form. Then run a test booking from the customer view.

Look at the whole path, not just one screen. The deposit should charge during checkout, the booking should not count as final until payment clears, and the appointment should show in the calendar with the right status. If the software marks it confirmed before the deposit hits, that is not a deposit flow. That is wishful thinking.

Show the deposit amount clearly on the booking page

Customers need to see the deposit amount before they hit submit. The page should also explain what the deposit covers and that a balance remains due later. Hiding those details until after payment just creates confusion, and nobody needs extra customer support tickets as a hobby.

Keep the wording plain. A customer should spot at a glance how much gets charged now and what still needs paying after the service.

Set the cancellation and refund wording next to the deposit

This is the spot for your policy language. If you cover refunds, cancellation fees, notice periods, or rescheduling terms, double check that the text matches your written policy.

On the legal side, keep it tight and consistent. The booking page is not the place to invent policy on the fly.

Worked example: a simple service booking with a required deposit

A customer books an online consultation service. On the booking page they see a deposit line item before checkout, plus a note that the remaining balance gets collected after the service finishes.

They enter their details, pay the deposit, and the booking confirms. The calendar shows the appointment as booked, and the business gets the payment record right away. Later the remaining balance is collected through the business’s usual process, whether that is in person, by invoice, or via a follow-up payment link.

That is the whole trick. The deposit locks in the booking, and the rest of the money shows up later, ideally without anyone claiming they “meant to send it yesterday.”

Check the confirmation message and admin workflow

After setup, check the customer confirmation message and the internal admin flow. The customer should receive a clear receipt or confirmation that lists the deposit paid and the appointment details. Staff should see the payment record, booking status, and whether the appointment is marked as partially paid.

Also confirm who gets internal notifications. If the front desk, service provider, and owner each need different alerts, set that up before launch, not after the first chaotic Monday morning.

FAQ

Can you take a deposit without charging the full amount upfront?

Yes. A deposit workflow collects only part of the total at booking, with the rest handled later according to your policy.

Should every online booking require a deposit?

Not necessarily. It depends on the service, the no-show risk, and how you want to balance convenience with payment protection.

What should staff do if a deposit payment fails?

Stop the booking, ask the customer to retry, or hold the appointment until payment is completed. The key is not to treat an unpaid booking like a finished one.
